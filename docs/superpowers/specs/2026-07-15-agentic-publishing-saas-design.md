# Alomat Agentic Publishing SaaS Design

## Purpose

Reposition Alomat LLC from a broad intelligent-systems studio into a focused,
global managed SaaS company for digital publishers. The public story must make
clear that Alomat is not a news publisher or a generic content-writing tool. It
builds and operates an agentic publishing platform that turns trusted sources
into publish-ready content while preserving editorial judgment, house style,
and human approval.

The repositioning must unify the real capabilities already demonstrated by
`ai-xabar-agent`, `haber-ozet-bot`, and `Xushnudbek-agent` without presenting
those repositories as three unrelated products. They are evidence and product
prototypes for one shared platform.

## Approved Commercial Direction

**Category:** Agentic publishing operations platform

**Business model:** Managed SaaS

**Initial market:** Global digital publishers, independent media teams, and
publisher-led channels

**Primary promise:** Turn trusted sources into publish-ready content.

**Supporting line:** Agents monitor and verify sources, learn editorial context,
prepare text and visuals, and route every output through accountable human
approval.

The initial wedge is source-to-publish-ready workflow automation. Editorial
voice learning and multi-channel operations are differentiators inside the
product, not competing primary messages.

## Product Narrative

Alomat gives a publisher a managed editorial operating layer. During onboarding,
Alomat configures approved sources, editorial rules, archive-backed context,
output formats, model routing, review roles, and delivery destinations. The
customer then receives a repeatable operating workflow rather than a collection
of prompts or a bespoke bot they must maintain.

The product loop is:

1. **Monitor:** Watch approved feeds, sites, channels, and submitted URLs.
2. **Verify:** Extract primary material, group related coverage, detect
   duplicates, and preserve source references.
3. **Understand:** Score relevance, retrieve editorial memory, choose the
   appropriate content format, and apply the publisher's rules.
4. **Produce:** Draft text, summaries, visual direction, and channel-ready
   packages through specialized agents.
5. **Review:** Present traceable output to a human editor for approval, editing,
   rejection, or escalation.
6. **Learn:** Record decisions and outcomes so the workflow and evaluations can
   improve without bypassing human accountability.

## Shared Platform Capabilities

The public product architecture is organized into six modules:

1. **Source Intelligence** — continuous monitoring, URL intake, extraction,
   source prioritization, related-story grouping, and failure tracking.
2. **Editorial Memory** — archive retrieval, style guidance, duplicate
   detection, topical context, and publisher-specific constraints.
3. **Agent Workflow** — specialized research, writing, editing, visual, and
   verification stages coordinated as one observable pipeline.
4. **Content Packaging** — short updates, deeper explainers, digests, summaries,
   and visual briefs adapted to the selected publishing surface.
5. **Human Control** — approval queues, edit/reject paths, source visibility,
   escalation rules, and explicit hard stops for unsafe or promotional content.
6. **Operations & Evaluation** — delivery state, retries, traces, quality gates,
   review feedback, and workflow-level monitoring.

These modules describe one product. AI agent engineering, multilingual
technology, and full-stack delivery remain technical capabilities behind the
platform, not separate top-level businesses.

## Evidence and Claim Boundary

Alomat may state that the system is in production with an active digital
publisher. The customer must remain anonymous on public surfaces and must not
be described by country, language, audience size, logo, revenue, or identity.

Approved proof language:

- "In production with an active digital publisher."
- "Built from live publishing workflows, not a prompt demo."
- "Production-tested patterns include continuous source monitoring,
  archive-backed editorial memory, human approval, and publish-ready delivery."

The site may describe three real deployment patterns without naming projects:

1. **Always-on signal desk:** monitors multiple sources, groups stories,
   prepares formats, and waits for owner approval.
2. **URL-to-content package:** turns an approved article URL into a concise
   summary and visual package.
3. **Editorial memory system:** retrieves historical style and topic context,
   routes work through specialist agents, and applies editorial quality gates.

The site must not claim unverified customer counts, time savings, publication
volume, revenue, funding, partnerships, accuracy percentages, or guaranteed
outcomes. Former workplaces remain team experience, not Alomat customers.

## Managed SaaS Delivery Model

The offer is service-assisted software, not an open-ended custom development
engagement.

### Managed onboarding

- approved-source and connector setup
- archive import and editorial-memory preparation
- output formats and brand rules
- review roles and approval boundaries
- delivery-channel configuration
- evaluation examples and acceptance criteria

### Recurring SaaS operation

- continuous source and workflow operation
- managed agent/model routing
- approval queue and delivery state
- observability, retries, and quality gates
- periodic evaluation and workflow refinement

### Optional enterprise work

Custom connectors, private deployments, advanced integrations, and additional
review requirements may be sold as scoped add-ons. They must not replace the
platform narrative or make Alomat appear to be a general software agency.

## Domain and Brand Architecture

- `alomattech.com` is the corporate, product, trust, diligence, and conversion
  surface. It explains the SaaS, production evidence, managed onboarding,
  company identity, and contact path.
- `alomat.ai` remains the public editorial product laboratory and demonstration
  surface. It proves that Alomat understands signal selection and reader-facing
  editorial experience. It must not be presented as the only product or as the
  corporate homepage.
- Alomat LLC is the company. `.alomat` is the visual brand. The first SaaS
  release uses the descriptive category rather than inventing a second product
  brand prematurely.

## Website Information Architecture

### Global navigation

The primary navigation becomes Product, Workflow, Deployments, About, and FAQ.
The conversion action becomes `Request a managed pilot` and uses the existing
company email until a qualified intake form is intentionally introduced.

### Home page

1. **Hero:** Establish the category and primary promise. The headline is
   "Turn trusted sources into publish-ready content." Supporting copy describes
   a managed agentic publishing platform with editorial memory and human
   approval. The signal-field visual is retained but relabeled around the
   product workflow.
2. **Production proof strip:** State that Alomat is in production with an active
   digital publisher and identify the four evidence dimensions: live sources,
   editorial memory, specialist agents, and human approval.
3. **Platform modules:** Explain the six product modules with concrete outcomes.
4. **Workflow:** Show the six-stage monitor-to-learn operating loop.
5. **Deployment patterns:** Present three anonymous, factual deployment patterns
   derived from the existing systems.
6. **Managed SaaS:** Explain onboarding, recurring operation, and customer
   responsibilities. The message is "your editorial system, operated with you."
7. **Final conversion:** Invite a publisher to request a managed pilot via
   `hello@alomattech.com`.

### Product page

Create `/platform` as the canonical detailed product page. It explains the
problem, module architecture, workflow, control boundaries, deliverables,
deployment model, and who the platform is for. Organization and Software
Application structured data must remain factual and avoid prices or ratings.

### About page

Reframe Alomat as a product company. Team experience in voice, translation,
full-stack product development, and agent engineering remains supporting proof.
The page must not lead with consultancy or studio language.

### FAQ page

Answer buyer and reviewer questions about product scope, managed onboarding,
sources, editorial memory, human approval, supported output surfaces, data and
deployment boundaries, and the managed pilot.

### Existing capability pages

Keep the existing technical pages available for search continuity, but rewrite
their descriptions so they support the publishing platform. They should explain
technical building blocks and must not compete with `/platform` as independent
service offers.

## Program and Incentive Narrative

Program applications must describe real technical work rather than optimize for
credits or imply automatic eligibility. The reusable technical narrative is:

- multi-source ingestion and resilient extraction
- multilingual retrieval and editorial-memory systems
- multi-agent orchestration with deterministic controls
- source verification and duplicate detection
- human-in-the-loop review and escalation
- text and visual generation pipelines
- workflow evaluation, traces, fallbacks, and observability
- multi-tenant productization and managed onboarding

Applications may cite an anonymous active production deployment when allowed by
the program. Every provider's current eligibility, terms, credit restrictions,
and required evidence must be verified from that provider's official materials
before submission. Alomat must never invent employment history, customers,
metrics, or usage to qualify.

## Visual Direction

Retain the technical-editorial identity: warm off-white canvas, near-black type,
coral signal, rigorous rules, oversized typography, sparse but specific copy,
and the existing `.alomat` mark. The visual metaphor changes from a generic
signal-to-system graph into a publishing operations trace.

The memorable interface moment is a continuous coral route across the product
loop: source, verify, memory, compose, review, delivery. The site must avoid
generic SaaS dashboard mockups, purple gradients, floating AI cards, stock
photos, fake customer-logo clouds, and speculative metrics.

## Technical Architecture

The site remains a static Astro application with strict TypeScript and typed
content. `src/content/site.ts` remains the canonical public-claim source.

- Home components own hero, proof, platform, workflow, deployments, managed
  SaaS, and final CTA sections.
- `/platform` uses typed module and workflow data from the same source.
- Existing legal pages remain tracker-free and form-free.
- The email conversion uses an explicit subject line for managed-pilot requests.
- Organization, WebSite, FAQPage, Service, and SoftwareApplication structured
  data must mirror visible, factual content.
- Content remains complete without JavaScript and all motion respects
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Testing and Release Gates

Development follows test-first content and behavior contracts.

- Unit tests assert the approved category, promise, six modules, six workflow
  stages, anonymous production proof, managed SaaS language, and prohibited
  claims.
- Browser tests assert the new hero, production proof, product navigation,
  platform page, managed-pilot conversion, mobile navigation, and reduced-motion
  experience.
- Existing legal, SEO, no-JavaScript, and responsive tests remain green.
- `npm test`, `npm run check`, `npm run build`, and `npm run test:e2e` must pass.
- Desktop and mobile screenshots must be visually inspected before release.
- Public LinkedIn, GitHub, and deployed website copy must be compared against
  this specification before completion is claimed.

## Release Scope

This release includes the product repositioning, detailed platform page,
anonymous production evidence, managed SaaS offer, updated About and FAQ copy,
technical capability-page alignment, LinkedIn company copy, GitHub organization
copy/profile, metadata, structured data, tests, and deployment through the
existing Cloudflare Pages pipeline.

It excludes a self-service account system, customer dashboard, pricing table,
billing, analytics tracker, CRM, automatic publishing claim, public customer
identity, AWS account work, and provider-program applications.
